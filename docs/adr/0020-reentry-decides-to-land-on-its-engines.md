# reentry decides for itself to land on its engines

At block start, once #823 has said what the Entry would drop and #826 which chutes it would open, `reentry` predicts the touchdown with that drop and those chutes. At or under 10 m/s it flies on its chutes and drops as ADR 0018 says. Over 10 m/s with an engine that has fuel, it keeps everything, drops nothing, predicts again for the craft as kept, and lands on the engines under its Chute Group, unless landing legs stand above its shield (below). Over 10 m/s with no such engine it takes the chute path, and #827 decides what else opens. Flight m20261106-203509-34a6 predicted "powered finish, 24.1 m/s" and its script stayed on chutes, since only the script's `touchdown` option could choose the engines. The player chose this rule on 2026-11-06.

- **`touchdown` is removed.** Giving it fails with a reason that says it is automatic, at the Script Check for an inline lexicon and in flight otherwise. `chutes: "none"` with an engine aboard lands on the engine.
- **Two speeds.** 10 m/s is this decision's threshold, allowing for prediction error (#682 is open). 15 m/s is the limit the Craft Check judges by and the block fails on, on engines too: the 6 m/s limit `touchdown: "powered"` had is gone, since a craft short of fuel would otherwise fail for that alone.
- **The threshold is 15 m/s on Kerbin, Eve and Laythe (#840).** A stack kept through an entry there is dangerous: #557's flight m20261030-205112-0de6 reached 93.8% of the heat limit with its stack on, and the retry was Lost flying engine first. So where the chutes can land the craft under the 15 m/s failure limit with the stack dropped, they do, and the engines take over only above 15 m/s, where the alternative is a crash. Every other body keeps 10 m/s. The bodies are `Entries.KeepsStackDangerous`, beside the hot-entry list (`Entries.HotWithNoShield`: Eve, Laythe) plus Kerbin, and the Craft Check's walk reads the same `EntryFinish.ThresholdFor`. The cost, accepted by the player: 10 m/s left room for the prediction's error (#682), and on these bodies that room is gone, so a prediction just under 15 m/s can land over the failure limit.
- **Lighting.** The block activates the engines part by part and fires no stage: `reentry` still never stages. It lights every engine with fuel in one stage, whatever the current stage, as one of a cluster would turn the craft. That stage is the highest whose fuelled engines can stop the fall (#842): their thrust at the body's surface (a vacuum engine counts for its low thrust there) at least 1.5 times the weight of the craft as kept. Core has no braking figure of its own, so 1.5 is the rule. With no stage able it is the one with the most thrust, and a tie goes to the higher stage. A weak engine in the top stage, an ion or a small vacuum engine, therefore no longer locks out the main engine below it. The log line names the stage, and why when it isn't the highest. If those give no thrust it lights the other engines that have fuel at once, and with still none it takes the chute path. If those flame out while braking it lights the others then.
- **What counts as fuel.** Stock's flow (`Part.GetConnectedResourceTotals`, per propellant), so crossfeed and fuel lines count and no tank is summed. A throttle-locked engine doesn't count: a solid motor can't brake a landing, and lighting a Sepratron would fire it at once.
- **A landing always drops what is below its legs (#940, the player, 2026-11-08).** With parts tagged `landing_leg` on the home side of the Entry Shield, the stack below it drops on its normal schedule, powered finish or not, as `land` drops its Underhang. `EntryFinish.For` then decides for the craft without the stack: only its engines, fuel and weight count, `KeptSpeed` is the craft that stays, and the engines that stay land it, chutes included; with none staying it is the chute path. `reentry` reads `keeps_stack`, not `powered`. Flight m20261108-220659-ac85 kept 53 t to land on the launch stack's Mainsail and was Lost, though its lander had an aerospike and legs above shield 2. A stack the Entry can't drop (no decoupler, or it holds the root or the part running the script) stays as before, as does a script's `separate: "none"`. The 10 and 15 m/s thresholds don't change, and with no legs above the shield nothing here does.
- **Shield.** A powered entry that keeps its stack sets `separate` to `"none"`, so a shield with anything below it stays on, as its decoupler takes both sides. One with nothing below it, including one whose stack dropped because legs stand above it, goes at `reentry_shield_due`'s timing, or as braking starts if that comes first, before the engines fire. That holds on every Entry, the chute path too, so the shield is gone for the liftoff that follows (the player, 2026-11-08). On every Entry a decoupler between the shield and the part above it fires first (Core names it, `ENTRYDROP`'s `jettison_first`, #940): a shield's own module is omni and would leave that decoupler on the engine, where it is no longer beside the shield and never fires. A shield with no decoupler above it goes as before.
- **Short of fuel or thrust.** It brakes with what it has, and only a touchdown over the limit fails the block. A fall the engines can't stop waits for the air first, on the shield's own speed and height: high on a shallow path thrust barely points up, and the fuel would otherwise go at orbital speed.
- **In Core, firing in Kerboscript (ADR 0014).** `EntryFinish.Decide` is the decision, reached through `EntryFinish.For` behind `ADDONS:CLAUDIA:ENTRYFINISH`, next to `CHUTEGROUP`. Braking, throttle and lighting stay in `reentry`. `keep_engines` and its walk (`EntryStack.Kept`) are deleted with their tests. The behaviour changes on purpose, so there is no parity test. The chute path in `Decide` is the seam #827 extends.

**The checks.** The Margin Check budgets `TouchdownSpeed` for a finish on any Entry but the return with a touchdown over 10 m/s and an engine aboard, as well as for any over 15 m/s, as before. Findings stay on 15 m/s: from 10 to 15 m/s with an engine the delta-v is carried in the margin and the Chutes line reads "powered finish", with no finding, as the chutes survive without the engines. No prompt text changes. The checks' walk follows the decision: an Entry that lands on its engines keeps its stack's chutes and engines, for itself and the Entries after, unless legs stand above its shield, where only the craft that stays counts, its mass and speed included (#940).

**Kept Blockers.** A Kerbin return its chutes can't cover stays a Blocker (#557: a stack on that entry runs close to the heat limit), though in flight the engines beat a crash. Chutes in one stage over two Entries stays a Blocker even when the first Entry lands on its engines, since that Entry opens the group all the same and the second has none. Only a script's `chutes: "none"` would save them, and the Craft Check can't see a script.

We rejected leaving the choice to the script, as the flight shows it gets missed, and keeping only the engines and the parts in line with them, as that dropped the radial tanks: about 3,264 of the flight's 3,338 m/s, and its stage-2 chutes.

Decided on #824 (2026-11-06).

**Dated note, 2026-11-09 (#954).** Flight m20261109-140705-ae3a braked at 1.7 km under chutes still opening and tumbled. The player chose chutes first, engines last, on a powered finish with chutes in its group: braking waits until every chute in the group is fully deployed and the canopies have done their slowing (the speed settled, or about 8 s fully open: a chute reading deployed is still filling), then starts at the brake height from the live speed. Under open chutes (and drogues) the wheels hold surface retrograde with RCS off and no lateral or site correction, and straight up under about 3 m/s with no drift term; #942's low-charge release still wins. Gimbals lock from fully open to touchdown and are given back on every exit, and no chute opens under a burn that began with them all out. A second Duna loss (m20261109-200745-96f6: an engine burning as the chutes opened, the spin going from 42 to 186 deg/s in a second, where the wheels give about 7 deg/s²) settled the rest: a spin has to be prevented, not corrected. So thrust is zero from the moment a chute is called until it reads fully open and settled, for an engine already burning and for each set in turn (drogues, then mains: the second set called zeroes it again), and no path lights the engine under a filling canopy. A fast fall with healthy chutes relies on them completely, which the player accepts: a stop from 140 m/s takes about 1,080 m with Duna's gravity. Only a failed chute is exempt: cut, missing, or still packed 1 s after it was first called, timed from the call and not from a height (a block starting at 1.5 km is already under the drogues' 3,000 m last call, and they are not failed on its first tick). With nothing filling and a chute failed, braking starts at the last-resort height, where full thrust with no margin can only just stop the fall, plus half a second of the fall for the sample (worked past the ground, so a fall already past it starts at once), once the air has done its work, on the shield's own speed and height as above. Failed or absent chutes must not mean a crash. On every powered finish thrust goes to zero over 20 deg/s of tipping (one named constant, to tune from the log; an unreadable rate doesn't hold the engine, as a reading that never comes must not stop the landing and the next guard still catches a tipped craft) and over 30° off the steering aim, so thrust doesn't pulse each time a spinning craft swings past its aim and the 10% upward floor can't raise it on a tipped craft. RCS stays off. The trace gives the rate and why the thrust is zero (chute opening, spinning or off aim). No chutes in the group, or `chute_mode` "none", brakes as above, apart from the guard. The chute-only path is unchanged. The player's answers on the same day settled four more points. The 20 deg/s rule is tipping only, measured as `land` does, leaving out roll about the thrust axis. A last resort once burning isn't interrupted by a later, different set being called, though the tipping and 30° rules hold throughout and a chute that counted as failed still zeroes the thrust if it opens after all. A chute armed for over a second still counts as failed, and is on the in-game watch list. And if the addon can't read the chute states, on a powered finish only, for about a second without a break (one bad tick cuts nothing), `reentry` cuts the chutes that are out, calls no more, frees its gimbals and lands on its engines: radar braking under a canopy is gone. A chute-only descent reads no states and is unchanged. The decisions are in Core (`ReentryBrake`, `BRAKEHEIGHTS`, `BRAKETICK`, ADR 0014); steering, throttle and gimbal commands stay in `reentry`.

**Dated note, 2026-11-09 (#958).** Flight m20261109-200745-96f6 lost a heat shield and the 23 parts below it at 16 km, mid-burn. `abort_on_part_loss` ended `reentry`, and the craft that was left fell for 66 s with no script while Claudia planned. It still had a working Dart and all its chutes. The player's rule: while `reentry` falls through the air (at or under the air top, off the ground), a part loss doesn't end the block, whatever `abort_on_part_loss` says. Above the air, and in every other block, the shared check stands. On the tick a loss is seen, Core's `ENTRYLOSS` re-reads `ENTRYFINISH` for the craft as it now is (nothing more to drop) and `CHUTEGROUP` for the engine it decides. A powered finish stays on its engine while it has thrust: a burn under way isn't handed to chutes that would now do. With no thrust left, the finish's answer decides, so a chute path that loses its chutes but keeps an engine lights it (part by part, no stage) and takes up its hold, and a powered finish that loses its engines goes to whatever chutes are left. With the engines gone, `chute_mode` comes back to the script's own, so a group widened for a craft with no engine opens, even after #954's cut. One exception covers a group with a chute already called or out (armed, semi or deployed, as the addon reads it for `BRAKETICK`). Both readings count only stowed chutes, so they would miss it: a called chute still packed would drop out, the group could widen to a later stage's chutes, and an empty group would let an engine burn as the canopy fills. In that case the group stays as it is and a chute path stays on its chutes. Otherwise the group is the fresh read, and a chute set with a stowed chute in it is called again. A burn whose engines went may call chutes under it, and at the end it gives the controls back as any burn does (`powered or braking`). With nothing left to slow it, the block flies to the end. #954's held times need no reset: a fresh group's `uids` are re-read, so a lost chute is no longer counted as failed, and its call and open times stay with the chutes left. A kept group still holds a lost chute's uid and counts it as failed (missing), as #954 does, so a lost chute never holds the thrust at zero to the ground. The block's own drops are told apart by the part count. The stack drop (`claudia_fire_drop`) and the shield drop each take the craft as it is once the parts have left, before any tick counts them, and a chute cut leaves its part aboard. A loss inside the shield drop's half second therefore goes unnoted. The result carries `parts_lost`, and a `parts_lost` blocknote gives each one; the Debrief's run events still name each part. Decisions in Core (`BlockCalls.EntryPartLoss`, ADR 0014); lighting and steering stay in `reentry`.

**Dated note, 2026-11-09 (#963).** #958's exception kept a chute path on its chutes whenever a chute of the group was called or out, so one that lost its mains under its drogues fell on at drogue speed with an engine aboard. The `chosen` rule in `BlockCalls.EntryPartLoss` now has one more case: on a chute path with a chute out, if a chute of the group is gone and no main is left aboard uncut (the addon's `drogue` reading tells them apart), `ENTRYFINISH`'s fresh answer decides, as it does with no chute out. Engines: the block lights them and flies the powered finish with all of #954's rules. Chutes (no engine with fuel, or none that will light): it stays on the drogues. The `parts_lost` note's `why` begins "mains lost, on to the engines" or "mains lost, staying on the drogues". A main still aboard, in any state, keeps the chute path, so a canopy the craft is counting on is never flown under by an engine. The group is still kept, not re-read, so `BRAKETICK` sees the drogues that are out or armed and holds thrust at zero until they are fully open and settled; the lost main is missing there, so it counts as failed and holds nothing back. The engine can't burn before that word: `reentry_light` now sets the pilot's throttle to zero before lighting anything, and the throttle is locked only once braking starts. At any switch to the engines in the air the addon resets what `BRAKETICK` holds between ticks (`BlockCalls.EmptyBrakeHold`): a chute path never asks it, so the held times would otherwise be an earlier flight's. A set already out is then called at the switch, and its settle time runs from there, never shorter than its own. The result reports the switch through `powered_dv` and the note; a result field of its own would change the `//|` docs. No library line was added.

**Dated note, 2026-11-10 (#969).** Flight m20261109-200745-96f6 (Duna, 41 t kept with `separate: "none"`) lit the last resort at 2,872 m because its drogues, called at 692 m/s, couldn't open over 500 m/s. That burn did its job: the drogues and then the mains opened. But a last resort, once begun, braked to the ground: about 110 s at about 0.1 throttle (the Mainsail is about 12 times the craft's weight) under a canopy swinging about ±30° every 10 s, cut 11 times by the 30° rule, reaching 73° and 46 deg/s at the end; the Mainsail was lost about 22 m up, touchdown 7.5 m/s, `powered_dv` 511. The player's rule: once the chutes are open, the burn stops and the engine waits for the final burn, cutting the chutes as it starts, so it saves delta-v and nothing swings under the burn. `BRAKETICK` now has a final stage. Braking (a last resort, or radar braking that began under open chutes) with every chute aboard reading fully open and settled (`ChutesSettled`, from when they all first read so; a set passed over by the last resort counts here) gives zero thrust (`zero_why` "final burn wait") and waits for the final height: `ReentryBrake.FinalHeight`, the brake height worked out with no drag at all, as the chutes will be cut. That wait holds until no chute is left, whatever a chute or the slowing then reads, since the height assumes no drag anyway. At that height, within 30° of upright, the block cuts the chutes (blocknote `chutes_cut`, `why` "final burn"), frees its gimbals and flies the final burn on surface retrograde, the 20 deg/s and 30° rules unchanged. More than 30° off upright there, or tipping faster than 20 deg/s (a craft swinging through upright would lose its canopy just as the spin rule zeroes the thrust), the cut is held and the thrust stays zero ("tipped, cut held") until the craft is within 30°, unless the craft is under the height where full thrust with no drag only just stops it: there it burns with the chutes on, on the guards, as cutting or waiting would leave it falling. With no thrust there is no final height, and nothing is cut. Falling under chutes open and settled, braking now starts at the final height when that is above the sampled brake height (which counts the canopy's drag), so radar braking under a canopy is also a late burn with the chutes cut. The first tick of the wait is noted (`thrust_stopped`, with why, the height, the speed and the final height); the addon writes that note itself, so the library gains no line. A chute-only descent never asks `BRAKETICK`, so never cuts this way. #963's switch to the engines, #960's semi-then-cut and #968's ground settle are untouched. Decisions in Core (`ReentryBrake.FinalHeight`, `FinalStep`, ADR 0014); the cut and the throttle stay in `reentry`.

**Dated note, 2026-11-10 (#969, the player's option c).** A ±30° swing every ~10 s passes upright at about 19 deg/s, inside the 20 deg/s cut rule, and the final height left only a second of the fall and a sixth of the thrust: about 19 m, 1.5 s, on the Mainsail stack, whose wheels (about 7 deg/s²) let it turn another 20 to 30° before they stop it. The 30° guard could then zero the thrust near the ground with the chutes gone. So the final height now adds the fall while the craft stops its turn: `ReentryBrake.TurnStopSeconds`, the tipping rate at the cut over the wheels' angular acceleration, plus, if stopping (taken as turning away from upright from the angle off upright at the cut) would carry it past 30°, the time to come back inside 30° from rest. The acceleration is `TurnTime.Alpha` on `TurnCraftReader`'s reading, the path TURNTIME uses, wheels only: RCS is off under the chutes, and the gimbals and RCS the burn may add only make it quicker. The addon reads the craft for `BRAKETICK` itself, only while the craft is tipping within the cap's reach of the final height, so the library gains no line; a read that fails is logged and adds nothing. No turn (or an unread rate) leaves the height as before. The cap is 6 s (`MaxTurnStopSeconds`): the worst turn the cut allows, 20 deg/s at 30° turning away, takes the 41 t stack about 5.7 s, and at a settled canopy's speed (about 8 to 12 m/s) 6 s is under 100 m higher, never kilometres. A craft whose turn would take longer gets the capped height, and there the cut is held ("tipped, cut held", thrust zero) until its swing slows; under the height where full thrust with no drag only just stops it, it burns with the chutes on, on the guards, as a tipped craft already does. The 20 deg/s and 30° rules are unchanged. The tick that cuts gives a blocknote `final_cut` (height, final height, rate, angle off upright and `turn_s`, the seconds added), written by the addon. No `//|` or prefix change.
