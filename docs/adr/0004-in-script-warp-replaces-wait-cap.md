# In-script warp replaces the wait cap

A Maneuver Sequence that must wait for a time-based event (a transfer window, daylight at a landing site, a surface alignment, a phase angle, a hold of a few orbits) now warps on rails to it inside its own Mission Script, however long the wait. This reverses part of the Mission timing design (#63): waits inside a sequence were capped at one orbit (3 h on the ground, `wait_cap_orbits` and `wait_cap_ground_s` in the player config), the Core Library failed a block that would wait longer, and the longer wait became a Due Time that the player crossed by pressing Warp to Due Time and then approving the next sequence. In play (Mission m20261025-041623-b4c8) that meant warping by hand to a Due Time for nothing but a window, and the player asking Claudia to "replan with timewarp". The player's Approval of a sequence now approves its wait as well; the mod adds no separate warp step.

- **No wait cap.** `claudia_check_wait`, `claudia_wait_cap` and the bundled `claudia_config()` are gone, and the mod no longer reads `wait_cap_*` (an older `config.cfg` that still has them is fine: unknown names are ignored). The Core Library's wait (`claudia_warp_to`, with `claudia_wait_until` as its 30 s form, and the `coast` block for a Mission Script's own holds) sets the warp mode to rails, warps to the wait's end less its lead, and is back at 1× at least 30 s before it, so a burn always starts off warp. `land` looks up to a day of the body ahead for a daylight pass over a site, and `transfer_to`'s window `next`, `return`'s window and its wait to leave the moon's sphere of influence warp there with no cap.
- **Due Time only for the player.** A Due Time is kept for a wait *between* sequences that needs the player, such as "come back when you've docked" or an EVA by hand. Claudia's prompt and the Mission Plan and Sequence Plan rules (the `due_time_ut` descriptions) say so. The Due Time machinery (the alarm, Warp to Due Time, Go to *vessel*, a missed Due Time) is unchanged.
- **Interrupts.** While a script warps, warp drops to 1× when the Mission needs the player, and the mod tells the script to hold it there (`ADDONS:CLAUDIA:WARPHOLD`). A Pause, the Spending Cap's waiting call, an Abort recommendation the player hasn't answered (#120; never offered in a Blackout) and Manual Control (which ends the run) hold warp for as long as they last. Link loss and a stock alarm firing are moments: they drop warp and hold it for 10 s of game time, and then the wait warps on. Otherwise a CommNet link that drops behind the Mun on every orbit would leave a 3-orbit hold crawling at 1×, and a Blackout doesn't need the player anyway. A player who cancels warp themselves is never overridden: the script asks for warp again only after a hold by the mod. *Changed by [ADR 0008](0008-physics-warp-on-coasts.md): the script asks again whenever the rate is 1×, unless warp is held or the player cancelled this wait (`ADDONS:CLAUDIA:WARPCANCELLED`).* *Changed by #417 (2026-10-27): link loss neither drops nor holds warp, on rails or physics, so the stock alarm is the only moment left. The player learns of the Blackout from the Mission window, not from warp stopping. Link loss during Manual Control with no other crew still takes the craft back (`LinkLostDuringManual`).* The runaway hard stop is unchanged and already counts the waits in the planned duration, so Claudia must count an in-script warp in `expected_duration_s`.
- **Runtime gates.** On-rails warp from a mod-started program stays allowed and physics warp stays refused (ADR 0003). *Physics warp on a Coast is allowed since [ADR 0008](0008-physics-warp-on-coasts.md).* kOS's `KUNIVERSE:TIMEWARP:WARPTO` and its `WARPTO()` function both call `TimeWarpValue.WarpTo`, which calls KSP's `TimeWarp.WarpTo`. That warp-to raises the rate through KSP's own `setRate` in whatever warp mode is already set and never changes the mode, so it would physics-warp past the `WARP` gate if the mode were PHYSICS (#133). The gates therefore also refuse `WARPTO` while the warp mode is physics. Source: kOS 1.6 `kOS.dll` (`kOS.Suffixed.TimeWarpValue.WarpTo`, `kOS.Function.WarpTo`) and KSP 1.12.5 `Assembly-CSharp.dll` (`TimeWarp.WarpTo`, `Update`, `autoWarpTo`), decompiled read-only.

**Amendment (a long coast can be left at a Coast Checkpoint, #775):** the first amendment's reason applies after a departure too: a coast of months to Duna should not hold the player, and a lost encounter found late costs far more than one found early. So during `transfer_to` or `return`, a coast of over 6 h (`WindowWaits.LongWaitS`) to the block's next correction point may stop at a Coast Checkpoint. This is automatic: no setting, no reply. Decided by the player in the grilling of 2026-11-05; the code is `CoastCheckpoints`, `TransferFlight` and `MissionControl.CoastCheckpoint.cs`.

- **The safety test gates every checkpoint.** The encounter is confirmed, the predicted periapsis is clear of the atmosphere and the surface, and no sphere of influence change comes before the next correction point (the target's own entry aside: the finer correction sits just inside it, and the safe periapsis covers a late return through it). When it fails, the block corrects or keeps flying, and a far switch stays an interruption, with a warning. So a `return` aimed into the air, its default, never checkpoints: a late return would lose the craft.
- **The block ends; the script doesn't carry on.** It ends Nominal and its result carries the next correction point and a resume token. A Mission's run gets the option that allows this (`checkpoint`, set when the script is bundled, only when the coast's block is the script's first and its options are inline); a block test or a Child Vessel's run never does. The kOS bridge ends the run at that block result, so nothing after the block runs; the resumed script is the same script, so what comes after still runs then. Kerboscript gets wiring only (ADR 0014).
- **The Due Time is a Due Time with one difference.** It is the correction point less the turn time and `BurnPoints.TurnMarginSeconds`, with the stock alarm and "Fly other Missions meanwhile". While the player is in the craft and has not left since, it is soft: one Approve starts the resumed sequence before it, and the script warps on rails to the correction with the Encounter Watch running. This narrows the rule above that the mod never starts a sequence before its Due Time, for this kind alone.
- **Auto Mode may stand in here and at no other kind of Due Time** (ADR 0009's rule otherwise stands). Auto Mode on with the player still in the craft starts the resume at once, through the same room check as any plan. A Blackout's own rules don't change: a Due Time still waits for the player there.
- **A far switch.** While the block only warps on such a coast, the guard offers "Leave at a checkpoint" and the block stops; it is the same checkpoint. Otherwise it warns and a switch is an interruption, as before. Leaving Flight during a run is still an interruption.
- **The check on return** runs as soon as the player is back on the craft by any way, with no Claude call, on the Encounter Watch's own rule. Kept: the resume stands, no request. Drifted or lost: Claudia plans from the figures. A late return takes the same two paths, the lateness among the figures; the safety test is why the craft is still there.

**Amendment (a window wait over 6 h is a Due Time, #616, decided in #489):** in-script warp covers waits of up to 6 h (one Kerbin day). A longer wait for a Transfer Window to another planet is a Due Time, so the player can fly other Missions meanwhile instead of watching a warp of months. *Changed by #647 (2026-11-02): a moon of the craft's planet has Transfer Windows too, and the rule is the same for its window: Minmus under Most efficient waits on the pad for a node.*

- **The rule.** The 6 h is fixed, not a Mission Setting. When the first Maneuver Sequence's Sequence Plan arrives (no run yet: the craft waits on the pad), the mod asks #458's solver (`TransferWindows`) for the window to the first planet the Mission Goal names, picked by Time versus delta-v as the Mission request's plan_transfer rows pick it. If the window less the launch lead (1 h: an ascent and up to one low Kerbin orbit) is more than 6 h off, that is the Sequence Plan's Due Time; a later Due Time of Claudia's own stands. A shorter wait stays in the script. Claudia's prompt is unchanged: she still puts window waits in the script, and the mod adds the Due Time itself (`WindowWaits`, `MissionControl.WindowWait.cs`).
- **How it shows.** The Missions list shows the Mission as Due with its day, the KSP Alarm Clock alarm is set at the Due Time (and moved or removed with it, behind the `IAlarmClock` seam), and the Capcom strip's status reads "Waiting for the Duna window: Y2 D140" until the Due Time. At the Due Time the Mission calls the player as any Due Time does. Auto Mode never stands in for the player there.
- **A missed window.** A player who approves after the window's departure gets (approving late inside the launch lead is not a miss: the window is days wide) the next window from the solver. If the Mission Plan's delta-v budget sum, with the next window's total in place of the missed one's, still has the Craft Check's margin under the craft's delta-v, the Due Time moves to it (or goes, if it is under 6 h off, and the script starts) and the player is told. The Craft Check is not run again. Otherwise the Sequence Plan is dropped and Claudia gets an Alert asking for a revised Mission Plan, or a plan with no sequences that proposes a reduced goal.
- **On another body's surface (#774).** A goal at a moon of another planet waits on the pad for that planet's window. A landed or splashed craft whose next legs are an ascent and a departure for another planet waits on the surface, at any point in the Mission: the ascent carries the Due Time, the departure less the ascent lead (`WindowWaits.AscentLeadS`: 600 s, one low orbit of the body, 900 s), or DeparturePlane's launch when that is earlier. The alarm, notice and missed-window handling are the pad's. A manual stretch comes first and has no Due Time; if the player hands back after the departure less the lead, the window it noted stands and approving the ascent is the missed-window handling, whose budget from a surface is the legs still to fly. A return to the craft's own planet has no window, so it gets none.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the cap and offer a warp button for the Due Time:** rejected. Every window wait would still cost the player a round trip (approve, warp, approve again) for a decision they had already made.
- **A separate warp step the mod runs between sequences:** rejected. It is a second Approval for the same wait, and the mod would need its own rules for when warp is safe. The Core Library already has them, because it drops to 1× before every burn.
- **Drop warp on link loss for as long as the link is down:** rejected for the reason above. The drop was kept as a moment, so the player still saw the Blackout begin. *Changed by #417 (2026-10-27): the drop and hold are gone. Link loss does nothing to warp, and the player learns of the Blackout from the Mission window.*
