# The arrival plane is set on the approach

A craft arrives in the plane and direction of travel the Mission needs by turning its approach, not its orbit. `transfer_to` aims the plane (`plane`, default `"auto"`) and records it; the `capture` block flies the arrival burn and whatever tilt is left. A plane change in low orbit is the dearest place for it: about 157 m/s for 10 degrees in low Duna orbit.

- **Plane on the approach.** A burn turns the arrival about its incoming direction for little, and only for that (B-plane targeting), so `CorrectionBurn`'s plane residual is one angle about the way in. Asking for the whole normal leaves the solve singular: it wants the approach itself to move, which costs far more (126 m/s at the Mun). To a far target the plane is trimmed inside the sphere of influence, with the periapsis, as #747 trims it. Inside the sphere of influence a burn can only reach planes through its own position, so `ArrivalPlane.Through` gives the closest of those.
- **A moon costs more than "a few m/s".** At the Mun the approach is slow (under 500 m/s) and the sphere of influence small, so the same trim costs 108 m/s for a polar orbit. The flight therefore aims the plane at the halfway Course Correction too, for about 40 to 80 m/s in all. That burn is big against the approach, and swings it some degrees, so a whole plane (a Target Vessel's or a body's) can come out of the trim 6 or 7 degrees off. `capture` takes that up, at about 8 m/s folded into the burn. At Duna the trim alone costs under 25 m/s for any plane through the approach.
- **Best-case budget.** The Delta-v Map and Craft Check add no plane allowance: `polar:` stays as it is, and the correction allowance and margin absorb the approach. The player noted that ignoring the plane has not cost a Mission yet. A moon's polar arrival is the case to watch: its 40 to 80 m/s can be more than the allowance.
- **Leftover rule.** `capture` burns a leftover tilt the cheaper of two ways: folded into the capture burn at periapsis, with what a burn there can't reach flown at a node of the low orbit; or capturing into a high ellipse, changing plane where it is slow, then lowering. A whole plane can only be reached at a node of the two planes, so "at its apoapsis" means the node farthest from periapsis; an inclination alone is reached at the apoapsis itself. Beyond `max_plane_dv` (the larger of 50 m/s and 5% of the capture burn) it captures in the plane it has and reports the leftover in degrees and m/s without failing. Claudia decides at the Debrief.
- **Direction rule.** A moon or vessel is matched as it goes: Minmus is 6 degrees, never the mirror 174, and a retrograde target gets a retrograde arrival. A number under 90 is prograde and over 90 retrograde. A landing site is prograde. The approach chooses the direction for free, by which side of the body it passes; a plane's sign is part of what `Through` and the residual aim for.
- **Who picks, in order.** Claudia's value; a Target Vessel orbiting the arrival body, at its periapsis altitude in place of `target_pe`; the next leg's landing site, as |latitude| + 0.5 degrees (an equatorial site sets no target); the next leg's moon of this body; none, as before. A trip that ends in reentry never has one.
- **`alt` default.** A Target Vessel's periapsis altitude when it orbits here, else the periapsis arrived at, raised to the Map's low orbit if it is under that. The brief said the usual low orbit; going down to it from a higher `target_pe` would spend burns against Claudia's own choice, and the Map's figure is for budgets: Minmus's, 4.2 km, is under its peaks.
- **The record.** Core records the plane in the Mission's save (`arrivalPlane`), so a `capture` in a later sequence, or after a Coast Checkpoint or a load, reads the plane `transfer_to` aimed for. With no record it works the plane out by the same rule. The plane is set in one place.

We rejected aiming the plane at the ejection (a change to `Transfer` and `SiblingEjection` for every Mission, when most have no plane to aim for) and a far target's mid-course burn (#747: out there a flown error of 0.05 m/s moves the periapsis tens of km). A direct intercept that times the arrival to leave the craft beside the Target Vessel is a separate ticket; returns home and aerocapture (#490) are out of scope.

Decided on #788 (grilled by the player, 2026-11-05). The logic is in Core under ADR 0014: `ArrivalPlanes`, `TransferFlight`, `CorrectionBurn` and `CaptureFlight`. `capture.ks` is wiring and steering only.
