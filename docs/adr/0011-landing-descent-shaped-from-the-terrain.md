# The landing descent is shaped from the terrain

`land` flew one fixed profile on every airless body. A deorbit burn lowered periapsis to a few km. Braking then flew a long, low glide, holding a height over the ground ahead out to where it expected to stop. Low passes over rims made braking start far out. In Mission m20261029-181119-0584 (Twin Craters, the Mun), braking started about 80 km from the site at 567 m/s, because the terrain ahead brought clearance under 1,500 m. The guidance then asked for almost no thrust. Speed was under the stopping curve, so nothing was asked along the track, and `land_split_accel` can't ask for downward thrust. The craft coasted up out of periapsis, swung its attitude chasing a tiny demand, and tripped "braking climbed 2 km" or "braking raised periapsis" on all three tries. Spike f093 on Minmus Flats failed the same way (#511). We now shape each descent from the terrain under its approach (grilled 2026-10-29; spec #511, plane changes #521).

- **The descent follows a Descent Shape.** Braking holds a reference height that depends on the fraction of horizontal speed left, not on the distance to the site: h_ref = h_end + (h_start − h_end) × (v_h / v₀)^shape. A small shape holds height until the craft is nearly stopped and then drops steeply. That's safe over rough ground but costs more delta-v. A large shape sinks early, much like the old glide, which is the cheapest profile. The curve ends at `h_end`, 200 m above the site's terrain, where the existing vertical terminal descent takes over.
- **The block picks the shape from a simulation, as launch flies `turn_shape`.** Before the deorbit burn, a block-internal call (ADR 0006, ADR 0007) simulates braking against the real terrain along the predicted track, allowing for the body's rotation. It models mass falling as fuel burns, the braking stage's thrust, the Underhang drop, and a TURNTIME turn delay. It searches periapsis heights, from the highest ground on the final stretch plus the margin up to 10 km over the site, against shapes from 0.2 to 3. It flies the pair with the least predicted delta-v whose path stays at least max(300 m, 2 s of descent rate) above the terrain. The search is capped at about 200 ms per pass. Unlike launch, the block works the shape out itself, because the terrain depends on the pass it picks at run time. Claudia may pass `descent_shape` after a failed attempt. The block flies it only if it clears, and otherwise refuses before the deorbit burn and reports the range that clears.
- **The approach is chosen for the whole landing, not only for reach.** `land` takes the soonest pass whose predicted landing delta-v is within 10% of the cheapest pass in the window. When no shape clears, the shared site search moves the site to the next-best one in the biome, or the nearest within 5 km of given coordinates, before giving up on a pass. `find_landing_site` proposes plane changes, as `change_orbit` calls Claudia flies before `land`, ranked by the plane change plus the predicted descent.
- **Braking starts where the simulation says.** The ground-close triggers (clearance under 1,500 m, or under 40 s to impact) fire only as a safety net, when clearance falls under the margin. At braking start the block predicts again from the real state. It switches to the cheapest shape that still clears, or doesn't brake at all and keeps its orbit, whose periapsis is above the terrain.
- **Guards measure against the prediction.** The block aborts when altitude is more than max(1 km, 25% of the predicted height) above the predicted path. The periapsis guard stays. The "climbed 2 km" clearance guard goes.
- **Steering stays near retrograde.** The commanded direction stays within 45° of surface retrograde. Under 5% of the engines' maximum acceleration, the block holds its last direction rather than turning toward a tiny vector.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the fixed low glide and only predict and guard it (#511 as first written):** rejected. A glide that begins at orbital speed and then needs no thrust has nothing to correct with, whatever its guard measures.
- **Always stop high and drop vertically:** rejected. It is the safest profile, but it pays for the fall and its gravity losses on every landing. On flat ground that is 100–200 m/s at the Mun for nothing.
- **A pitch program like launch's, instead of a height curve:** rejected. A height curve can be checked point by point against terrain and keeps the existing height-holding controller.
- **Claudia picks the shape with a Planning Tool, as for `turn_shape`:** rejected as the default. The terrain under the approach depends on which pass the block takes at run time, so a value from planning could be stale. She keeps an override for retries.
- **Let `land` change the plane itself:** rejected. A plane change is a large burn Claudia has to budget and the player has to approve. `land`'s correction burn stays capped by `max_correction_dv`.
- **Combine the plane change with the deorbit burn:** rejected. It saves only a few m/s and complicates the deorbit's timing.

The cost:
- **A second copy of the guidance.** The simulation runs the braking laws in C#, which must match the kOS ones. A test pins them together.
- **More delta-v over rough ground.** Steeper shapes over cratered sites cost more, and the pre-deorbit delta-v check now uses the simulated figure times `burn_margin`.
- **Planning and pre-burn time.** About 200 ms per pass in `land`, and up to about 2 s in `find_landing_site` across candidate planes. `land` spends that 2 s too, once, when a site is out of reach and it fails naming the plane changes (#521).
